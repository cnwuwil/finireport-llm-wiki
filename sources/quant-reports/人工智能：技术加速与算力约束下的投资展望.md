# AI Summit Key Conference Takeaways

AI acceleration is inverting the structure of work — The most consistent message across nearly every session was the acceleration of AI technology and enterprise adoption creating a fundamental inversion in how work gets done. Cursor's Michael Truell noted the acceleration as developers have gone from being $5 - 1 0 \%$ more 研报添加微信 LCD123321Cproductive with early models to running $^ { 2 - 3 }$ 04-17  agents at a time and accounting for $80 \%$ of enterprise customers code development. Mythos signals a step-change in AI capability…and concerns — Anthropic's deliberate decision to withhold its most capable model, Mythos, from broad release, citing cybersecurity and other risks, was the conference's most-discussed event. Capacity constraints — Compute demand is insatiable through 2028 at minimum, with the infrastructure panel placing supply/demand rebalancing at 2029 at the earliest or potentially never. The most-discussed binding constraint was power, not silicon. Lightning AI's William Falcon quantified the demand inflection: a single million-agent business deployment implies 100x growth in inference demand and a 1,000x need for compute. CoreWeave has secured 3GW of contracted power to expand from 850MW. The constraints are clearly showing up in higher prices for compute as well as just general unavailability for workload capacity at scale.

Physical AI — Physical AI emerged as the conference's most consensus-forward investment theme, with deployments already underway and capital formation accelerating. # 研报添加微信 LCD123321Cad 04-17 AWS Keynote: Applied AI at Scale

We hosted Colleen Aubrey, Senior Vice President of Applied Solutions at AWS, for a keynote conversation moderated by Ric Spencer, Vice-Chair of Tech Investment Banking and Head of AI Infrastructure Banking at Citi. The session covered AWS's philosophy for deploying AI inside the enterprise, the evolution from productivity tools to agentic “AI teammates,” real-world implementation results across retail and healthcare, and the strategic imperative of identifying durable, complex problems as the true long-term opportunity in enterprise AI.

An operator’s perspective. Aubrey brought an operator's lens to the AI conversation, drawing on her $1 5 +$ years scaling Amazon's advertising business into a $\$ 303-40 B$ franchise. The throughline from ads to AI, she argued, is the same: value creation comes not from giving customers exactly what they ask for, but from building on their behalf and iterating through failure. The inflection point in both cases arrived when the product became compelling enough that customers tolerated its rough edges — a signal, she suggested, that the industry is now approaching with enterprise AI.

Philosophy and approach to applied AI solutions. The centerpiece of Aubrey's remarks was AWS's "AI teammates" framework — a vision in which hundreds of thousands of task-level agents are orchestrated into functional teams, effectively making every employee a manager of AI. This represents a deliberate departure from productivity-tool thinking; the goal is not to automate individual tasks but to fundamentally restructure how work gets done, collapsing traditional role boundaries across marketing, engineering, and finance functions. Notably, AWS has chosen to avoid desktop metaphors and anthropomorphized interfaces, betting instead on new collaboration patterns native to agentic AI.

Underlying all of it is a strategic question that Aubrey framed as the most important challenge in enterprise AI right now: identifying which problems are truly durable. The shift AWS is driving — getting AI to work in the business while people work on the business — only creates lasting value if it is applied to problems that won't be rendered moot by the next wave of tooling. Intermediate workflows that can be vibecoded away in the near term are not the target; the real opportunity lies in complexity that compounds over time, where regulatory friction, institutional knowledge, and high-stakes outcomes make human-AI collaboration 研报添加微信 LCD123321Cad 04-17 irreplaceable. For AWS, healthcare and drug discovery are early proof points of that thesis — sectors where the cost of being wrong is high enough that durable, trustbased AI systems will always have a role.

Implementation case studies. Aubrey used two concrete deployments to ground the vision. The Tumi Group implementation via Amazon Connect — spanning 10,000 agents and 30 million customers — delivered a $30 \%$ decrease in contacts per booking and a $2 5 \%$ lift in upsell, illustrating the commercial impact of consolidating on a single platform rather than stitching together point solutions. The second example, Amazon Health Connect, takes a horizontal approach across the full patient journey — from appointment scheduling through clinical documentation and billing — with a single AI teammate maintaining context endto-end rather than handing off between siloed tools.

Healthcare emerged as a near-term beachhead for AWS, and Aubrey was candid about why: the sector's complexity, regulatory requirements, and administrative burden create durable, hard-to-commoditize problems that reward deep technical investment. The demo highlighted a key design choice — the AI raises its hand to flag gaps rather than auto-populating fields, preserving clinician control while surfacing meaningful clinical insights. AWS sees regulatory moats, HIPAAcompliant infrastructure, and FDA navigation as long-term competitive advantages, with Amazon Biodiscovery signaling that the same agentic architecture is being extended into drug and antibody discovery.
