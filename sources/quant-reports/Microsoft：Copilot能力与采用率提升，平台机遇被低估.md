5) Margin Dynamics: What Impacts the Bottom-line From Here?

# MS View: Gross Margin Pressures From Infrastructure Build-outs and Component Prices Can Continue to be Offset by Low Opex Growth

Gross Margins Moving Lower… Consensus looks for gross margins of $6 7 . 8 \% / 6 6 . 9 \%$ in FY26/FY27, down from $6 8 . 8 \%$ in FY25, largely due to increasing data center capacity coming online while Azure AI and Copilot revenue continues to scale. On the back of our aforementioned higher capex estimates, we lower our gross margin 订阅研报添加微信 LCD123321Cadestimates (see Exhibit 36 ) due to higher data center components costs (and higher associated depreciation/amortization over time), with our model now conservatively assuming total company gross margin compression to $6 7 . 6 \% / 6 6 . 7 \%$ in FY26/FY27, with roughly mid-teens COGS growth in each year. Management mentioned during the F2Q26 call that Cloud gross margins were slightly better than expected at $67 \%$ as 'continued investments in AI that were partially offset by ongoing efficiency gains,' though guided F3Q26 Microsoft Cloud gross margin to be $\sim 6 5 \% ,$ down YoY, driven by 'continued investments in AI.'

On the operating margin side of the equation, despite our expectation for further gross margin compression, we expect that operating expense discipline can offset higher COGs, which could drive operating margin expansion and upside. Our current model assumes $7 . 6 \% / 7 . 5 \% / 1 2 . 4 \%$ operating expense growth in FY26 / FY27 / FY28, which is backed by headcount growth having remained largely stable and our job posting tracker not picking up any signs of a material rebound in hiring. During 订阅研报添加微信 LCD123321Cad F2Q26 earnings, CFO Amy Hood also guided F3Q 'operating margins should be down 04-21 slightly year-over-year.' Net, our expectation is that Microsoft can deliver operating margin of $4 6 . 2 \%$ in FY26, slightly above FY25's $4 5 . 6 \% ,$ , followed by $4 6 . 6 \% / 4 6 . 8 \%$ in operating margins in FY27/FY28, driving GAAP EPS growth of $2 6 . 0 \% / 1 2 . 4 \% / 1 7 . 8 \%$ over the same period. Related, as previously noted (see 2Q26 Preview – Expecting Two Steps Forward (23 Jan 2026)), the shape of Microsoft's GAAP EPS growth is largely impacted by the other income loss related to the accounting for Microsoft's OpenAI investment, driving last quarter's introduction of Non-GAAP EPS (ex-OpenAI loss impacts), which will likely be provided consistently going
